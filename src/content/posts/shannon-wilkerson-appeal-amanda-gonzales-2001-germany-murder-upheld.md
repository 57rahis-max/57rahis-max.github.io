<p>A federal appeals court has upheld the conviction and 30-year sentence of a former soldier for the 2001 killing of Army Pfc. Amanda Gonzales, who was 19 and about five months pregnant when she was beaten and strangled in her barracks room in Germany.</p>

<p>Shannon L. Wilkerson, 46, was convicted of second-degree murder by a jury in Pensacola, Florida, in May 2024, more than 22 years after Gonzales died. In <a href="https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/unpub/files/202413694.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">an unsigned opinion filed on Sept. 30, 2026</a>, a three-judge panel of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 11th Circuit rejected every argument in his appeal. The U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Northern District of Florida announced the ruling on Oct. 16, 2026.</p>

<h2>What happened in 2001</h2>

<p>Wilkerson and Gonzales were both stationed at Fliegerhorst Kaserne, then a U.S. Army base in Hanau, and lived in barracks buildings about 500 feet apart, according to the opinion, which summarizes the trial testimony. Gonzales was found dead on the floor of her room on Nov. 5, 2001. Prosecutors say she was killed on Nov. 3, 2001. There was no sign of forced entry, and the medical examiner found she had been strangled with something wide, such as a forearm.</p>

<p>Wilkerson, who was married to another soldier on the base, had been having an affair with Gonzales. According to the testimony, his wife threatened to leave him if the baby was his, and Gonzales threatened to tell their command about the affair. Adultery is a crime under military law, and the punishment could include demotion or being forced out of the Army. One soldier testified that Wilkerson said, “I promise you I’ll kill her if she goes to the command.” Wilkerson denied saying it.</p>

<p>On the night of the killing, guards at the front gate stopped Wilkerson from leaving the base because he was too drunk, according to the testimony. Around 3 a.m., he showed up in another soldier’s room carrying a borrowed gray sweatshirt and asked the soldiers there to say he had been drinking with them all night.</p>

<h2>The DNA evidence</h2>

<p>In 2022, the Army’s crime lab retested that sweatshirt with newer DNA software. Its analyst testified that a DNA mixture from the inside of the right forearm came from three people: the sweatshirt’s owner, Wilkerson and Gonzales.</p>

<p>Wilkerson’s experts disputed that. One found only “limited support” that Gonzales’s DNA was in the mixture, and another said the analyst should have called the three people “possible contributors.” Wilkerson testified that he was “positive” he did not kill her, even though he was very drunk that night.</p>

<h2>What the appeals court rejected</h2>

<p>Wilkerson argued that the trial judge should have let the jury hear about Chinu Kim, a soldier who lived next door to Gonzales and later killed a mechanic and the mechanic’s son in 2006. The judge kept that evidence out, and the appeals court found no error. The opinion notes that Kim and Gonzales were only acquaintances and that Kim, when questioned, denied any role in her death.</p>

<p>The panel also upheld the trial judge’s refusal to order more raw data from the Army lab, which the judge had called a “fishing expedition,” and the denial of a new trial.</p>

<p>It upheld the sentence too. The guidelines from 2001 suggested 210 to 262 months, but the trial judge imposed 360 months, calling the old range “too lenient” because it did not account for the unborn child, the victim being a fellow service member, or the way she died. The judge also found that Wilkerson lied when he testified that he did not kill her, which added to his guideline range.</p>

<h2>How the case was brought</h2>

<p>Wilkerson was later discharged from the Army. He was arrested in Florida in February 2023 on a first-degree murder charge, CBS News reported, and the jury convicted him of the lesser charge of second-degree murder. The FBI’s New York and Jacksonville field offices investigated, with help from the Army Criminal Investigative Division.</p>

<p>“Nothing can bring back the innocent lives taken by this defendant’s heinous crime,” U.S. Attorney John P. Heekin said in announcing the ruling.</p>
