<p class="has-text-color wp-block-paragraph" style="color:#b3261e"><strong>FACT CHECK — THIS CLAIM IS FALSE</strong></p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The headline above is the claim being checked, not a report of something that happened. There was no such arrest. The story began as fiction on World News Daily Report, a site whose disclaimer identifies its articles as satire and its characters as invented. This page explains where the claim came from.</p>
</div>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">If you have seen the headline about a San Diego zookeeper arrested for assaulting a 500-pound gorilla, it is not a news story. It started as fiction, on a website called World News Daily Report, whose disclaimer tells readers plainly that its articles are satire and its characters invented. Two fact-checking teams have followed it back to that source: <a href="https://leadstories.com/hoax-alert/2017/12/fake-news-zookeeper-not-arrested-did-not-try-to-molest-500-pound-male-gorilla.html" rel="nofollow noopener" target="_blank">Lead Stories</a> in December 2017, and <a href="https://africacheck.org/fact-checks/fbchecks/no-zookeeper-not-arrested-molesting-500-pound-gorilla-its-satire" rel="nofollow noopener" target="_blank">Africa Check</a> in June 2019.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What the story claimed</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The piece described a zoo worker accused of misconduct involving a male gorilla. It had everything a real report has: a named suspect, a location, a weight for the animal, quotes from officials. None of that makes it true. The names, the numbers and the quotes were written as part of the story. They are not facts about a real person or a real animal.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">How we know it is fiction</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Lead Stories published its findings on 3 December 2017. It traced the story to World News Daily Report and printed the site’s own disclaimer, which says it “assumes all responsibility for the satirical nature of its articles and for the fictional nature of their content.”</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Then it followed the trail. A site called ActionNews3 had copied the article out, but left the satire disclaimer off. Huzlers ran a version too, this one crediting World News Daily Report and carrying a satire notice of its own.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That is the whole mechanism, and it is worth seeing clearly. Take the disclaimer off a satirical article and what is left looks exactly like reporting — a place, a name, a quote, a number. The format survives the copying. The label does not. Everyone downstream sees only the format.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The photographs do not hold up</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Two images travelled with the story. Africa Check examined both in June 2019, and neither one supports it.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A reverse image search turned up the photograph of the man circulating online as early as January 2010 — well before the story existed, and with nothing to do with it. Africa Check also noted the picture had been visibly edited. It did not name the man, and neither will we.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The second photograph was captioned in the original as an animal at the San Diego Zoo. Africa Check identified him as Shabani, a gorilla who lives at a zoo in Japan and had drawn press attention some years earlier for reasons entirely unconnected to any of this.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">One date worth getting right</h2>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Africa Check refers to a 2018 article. But Lead Stories was already debunking the claim in December 2017, which means it was circulating by then at the latest. Nobody has pinned down the day it first appeared, and we are not going to guess at one.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This headline should not be shared as a factual arrest report. The claim comes from a story its own publisher labels fiction. Africa Check found that the man’s photograph had circulated years before the story and identified the pictured gorilla as Shabani in Japan. Neither image substantiates the alleged incident.</p>
